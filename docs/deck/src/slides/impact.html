Two datasets, kept apart. On the original dataset, the department's 55 recorded days, the 90th-percentile wait falls from 108.6 to 70.4 minutes when the same day sizes are re-planned by RAD-SMART in the twin, and the share treated more than an hour late from 22% to 6%. The busiest days gain the most: 130 to 70 minutes. This is a replay in the twin, not a live trial; the pilot measures it on real days. On the simulated dataset, over 12 weeks with standing slots, the 90th percentile falls from 82 to 49 minutes and lateness over an hour from 19% to 4%; on breakdown days the gain is 69%. The price is real: the evening runs about two hours later, still inside the department's hours; one new patient in five starts later, by less than half a day; and a patient who asks for a particular time gets it within 15 minutes 4% of the time, against 31% today, because a standing slot must be free on every day of the course.
