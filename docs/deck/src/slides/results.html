Two datasets, kept apart. On the department's own 55 recorded days, the wait that one patient in ten exceeds falls from 108.6 to 70.4 minutes when the same day sizes are re-planned by RAD-SMART in the digital twin, and the share treated more than an hour late from 22% to 6%. The busiest days gain the most: 130 to 70 minutes. On 12 synthetic weeks where each course keeps one standing slot, it falls from 82 to 49 minutes. This is a replay in the twin, not a live trial; the pilot measures it on real days. The price is real: the evening runs about two hours later, still inside the department's hours, and one new patient in five starts a little later, by less than half a day on average.
