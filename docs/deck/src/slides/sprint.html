Built and working in CARE

  • Course entry with one standing time, approved by a doctor
  • Day board: arrival, in room, out, live rule check
  • Breakdown re-planning under the department's rules
  • Requests for another time, decided by staff

Round 1 on 19 Sep · CARE app on 24 Oct · Sarvam layer and patient page on 25 Oct · live on Azure on 26 Oct

8 / 12

Since Round 1 on 19 September we built the department's two sets of written answers into the engine: one standing daily time per course, free on every treatment date; new patients start and finish before 17:00, with 150 minutes kept free for future new starts; their breakdown rule (up to 60 minutes everyone is treated the same day, longer and patients still at home are told not to travel); and roles (only a doctor enters or approves a course). On 24 October the engine became a working application inside CARE, and on 25 October we added the Sarvam AI layer, the patient phone page, QR check-in and plain-language explanations, with automated safety tests. On 26 October it went live on Microsoft Azure: one Docker image built on CARE's production image, with a managed PostgreSQL database and HTTPS, deployed by one script, and open for anyone to try with synthetic patients. 30 automated tests now pass. Testing found and fixed four problems: Kannada suffixes on times that the first time check missed, Konkani turning "the next working day" into "next week" (messages now name the date), an explanation using the proposed rather than the current time, and one ambiguous patient button. Not yet built: automatic WhatsApp sending through the Business API, spoken replies, Excel and prescription import, plain-language rule editing, and an oncology information system feed.
