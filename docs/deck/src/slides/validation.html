We tested the language step with the app's own settings, on synthetic text. Seven message templates with four sets of times each, in Kannada, Malayalam, Hindi and Konkani: all 112 translations kept every clock time, so none would have fallen back to English. Translating back into English and comparing with the source gave a median chrF of 76 to 80 by language; this is a rough check of meaning, so native speakers at the department will rate every template before the pilot. For 20 synthetic plans, Sarvam-105B's plain-words explanation passed the time check every time, with no invented time. Session-length prediction is off by 2.15 minutes on average on held-out synthetic sessions. Safety: a person approves every course and every change; hard rules are constraints in the optimiser and are re-checked by an independent checker; the language models never schedule, a translation that changes a time is discarded and an explanation that invents one is rejected; no names, hospital numbers or phone numbers are sent to Sarvam; and if Sarvam is unreachable, messages go in English and staff see the engine's own reasons. The pilot follows the DPDP Act with patient consent for messaging and goes to the ethics committee.
