Every session has a time, and a reason

Complex cases stay inside 10:00–17:00, preferably the 12:00–13:30 block; new starts finish by 17:00; MHRC patients are treated in their 16:00–17:00 slots and catch the 17:00 bus; public-transport patients are home by 21:00; inpatients, dormitory residents and people living nearby take the late evening; and the single breast board and ABC unit never clash with the CT simulator. Machine downtime

When the machine goes down

The department's rules: for downtime of up to an hour, nobody is sent home, every ongoing patient is treated the same day, and a few new starts may be deferred. Beyond two hours, new starts move to the next day (urgent palliative starts excepted) and some patients may be deferred. RAD-SMART applies these rules, re-plans the rest of the day with the fewest changes, messages only patients with at least 45 minutes' notice, and proposes every deferral to the oncologist or senior RTT for approval.

Two machines, three weeks, one TBI course

New starts balanced by minutes, not head-count

Total body irradiation is known a month ahead and takes two 90-minute slots a day, so RAD-SMART takes those minutes off the Versa HD's capacity on the TBI days and holds back exactly the new starts that would not fit. Splitting new patients equally by head-count, even with a manual taper before TBI, overloads the Versa HD and delays starts. The second machine's capability matrix is still to be agreed, so its eligibility here is a placeholder.

Machine learning

Predicting machine-minutes

Trained on 12,000 synthetic past sessions, a gradient-boosting model learns first-fraction time, imaging type, wheelchair and stretcher transfers, and faster delivery on the second machine, which an average table misses. The department records arrival and treatment-start times but not how long sessions take, so the pilot starts from its own estimate table, which already delivers most of the gain, and switches to the model once room entry and exit times build up.

About

What this prototype is, and is not

How it works

  • Predict: gradient boosting gives expected and cautious (P80) minutes per session; plans reserve the expected minutes.
  • Optimise: a two-stage mixed-integer model (HiGHS) places each session on a 2-minute grid; hard rules are constraints, preferences are costs; an independent checker re-verifies every hard rule.
  • Simulate: a Monte Carlo twin replays 300 days of late arrivals, overruns, no-shows, urgent starts, imaging holds and faults.
  • Explain: every placement carries reason codes; in the full system a Sarvam AI copilot explains them and messages patients in Kannada, Malayalam, Hindi or English. Tulu, which Sarvam's models do not cover, uses recorded Tulu voice notes with Kannada-script text.

Assumptions and limits

    Safety by design

    • Plans, re-plans and deferrals are proposals until a designated senior RTT or oncologist approves them.
    • No diagnoses, doses, images or notes are used. Urgency and eligibility come from clinicians.
    • Language models never make a scheduling decision and never see names or phone numbers.
    • Every plan, override, rule version and message is logged.

    Reproduce it

    Python 3.10+, open-source libraries only. Patients are generated on the fly; the twin's calibration comes from poc/data/department_profile.json, which holds only aggregates of the department's records. A full run takes about three minutes.

    cd poc
pip install -r requirements.txt
python run_poc.py
